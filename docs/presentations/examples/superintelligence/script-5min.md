# Superintelligence: where we are and what comes next

Speaker script · 5:00 · about 140 words per minute

## 1. Superintelligence: where we are and what comes next — 0:00–0:20 (20 s)

Good morning. In the next five minutes I want to answer one question: how close are we to superintelligence, and what should we do about it? I'll show where AI really stands today, what experts expect, and a simple way for us to prepare.

## 2. What we mean by superintelligence — 0:20–0:45 (25 s)

First, a definition, so that we are all arguing about the same thing. The philosopher Nick Bostrom put it this way: an intellect that greatly exceeds human performance in virtually all domains of interest. Not one narrow task. Not chess, and not only coding. Virtually everything we care about, from science to strategy.

## 3. Capability is compounding on several fronts at once — 0:45–1:30 (45 s)

So where are we today? Progress is compounding on several fronts at once. The compute used to train frontier models has grown four to five times every single year. On top of that, algorithms become two to six times more efficient each year, so the same hardware buys more capability. More than seven hundred million people now use leading AI systems every week. And in 2025, AI reached gold-medal level on International Mathematical Olympiad problems. None of these on its own is superintelligence. Together, they explain why the question has moved from science fiction to board agendas.

## 4. Experts keep pulling their forecasts forward — 1:30–2:15 (45 s)

Here is what the experts think, and how fast that view is moving. The same survey has asked thousands of AI researchers when there will be an even chance of human-level AI. In 2016 the answer was 2061. In the 2022 survey it was 2059. Then it dropped sharply, and by the 2024 survey it was 2042. In other words, the horizon shrank from forty-five years to eighteen in just eight years. The uncertainty is still very wide, and nobody should treat 2042 as a date. But the direction of travel is clear: sooner than people thought.

## 5. Today's frontier AI is not superintelligence yet — 2:15–3:05 (50 s)

Let's be precise about the gap that remains. On the left is frontier AI today. It solves olympiad maths, passes licensing exams in medicine and law, and AI agents now complete software tasks that take a person about half an hour. But it still fails in surprising ways that are hard to predict. On the right is superintelligence: better than the best people almost everywhere, acting reliably over long horizons, and perhaps speeding up AI research itself. Whether that future goes well depends on one thing above all: whether its goals stay aligned with ours.

## 6. Most AI researchers want safety work to move faster — 3:05–3:40 (35 s)

That is why the people building these systems are asking for more caution, not less. In the latest survey, seventy-two percent of AI researchers want risk research to be prioritised more than it is today. The median researcher puts a ten percent chance on outcomes as severe as human extinction or permanent disempowerment. That is not a fringe view held by a few critics. It is the middle of the field.

## 7. Four open problems will decide how this goes — 3:40–4:25 (45 s)

Four open problems will decide how this goes. Alignment: keeping goals and behaviour matched to what we intend, even as capability grows. Evidence: capability moves faster than proof that our safeguards actually work. Security: misuse in cyberattacks and fraud is not hypothetical; it is already being observed. And governance: twelve companies now publish safety frameworks, but the rules differ widely between countries. The point to remember is this: capability is no longer the only question. Control and fair access matter just as much.

## 8. Prepare for superintelligence without betting on a date — 4:25–5:00 (35 s)

So here is my recommendation. Prepare for superintelligence without betting on a date. Treat it as a planning scenario, not a prediction. Concretely, three things: review capability, cost and adoption signals every quarter; set our AI use policy, evaluations and incident response now; and back independent evaluation and common standards. I'd like us to hold the first horizon review at next quarter's leadership meeting. Thank you, and I'm happy to take questions.
